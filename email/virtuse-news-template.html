Bitcoin Holds Above $76K — Here's What Actually Matters

Bitcoin is consolidating just above $77,000, easing back from the $80,797 high it set on August 25 as attention turns to the Federal Reserve's September 15–16 meeting. Zoom out and the pullback looks small: BTC is still up roughly 24% over the past 30 days, from $62,600 on August 1. Price is also holding well clear of its 100-day EMA, now near $68,700 — a level that capped every rally attempt back in mid-August and currently sits more than $8,500 below spot, the mark of a market cooling off, not breaking down.

The longer-term signal is just as calm. At $77,253, Bitcoin is trading 1.19× its 200-week moving average of $64,856 — a level Virtuse's own 200 WMA chart still scores as the "Deep Value Zone," the same read it gave at every major bottom since 2015. Trading this far above its multi-year floor while still reading as cheap says more about how far Bitcoin has re-rated over the past decade than about any near-term risk. Dominance sits at 59.1% of total crypto market cap — just under the 60% line that marks a fully Bitcoin-dominated market, with Bitcoin still firmly in the lead.

What to watch: holding above roughly $76,900 keeps the bullish setup intact and opens the door toward $82,200, with a clean break there putting the high-$90,000s back in play; losing that level exposes the low-$70,000s. Either way, the Rainbow Chart's verdict hasn't budged — it still reads Bitcoin as "Basically a Fire Sale," its cheapest band, relative to its own 12-year growth trend.
