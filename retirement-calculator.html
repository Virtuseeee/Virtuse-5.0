How much Bitcoin do I need to retire?

It depends on three things: what you spend each year, how many years the money has to last and how fast Bitcoin grows. In the calculator's base scenario of 20% a year, spending €30,000 a year from age 50 to 85 takes about 3.4 BTC today (about €199,000). In the conservative scenario of 10% a year it is about 6.6 BTC.

How to retire on Bitcoin

  1. Buy on a schedule

    A fixed amount every week or month (DCA) instead of guessing the right day. The DCA calculator shows what fees take from a monthly plan.

  2. Hold your own keys

    Savings you won't touch for years can sit on a hardware wallet you control, with the recovery phrase written down offline. Compare hardware wallets.

  3. Keep a cash reserve

    Bitcoin has fallen 70% or more from a high four times since 2011. One or more years of spending in cash means you don't have to sell after a fall. Bitcoin returns by month.

  4. Set a withdrawal rate

    Decide how much you sell each year and test it in the calculator with the conservative scenario, not only the base one. A plan that only lasts in the optimistic scenario depends on the best case.

  5. Know the tax in your country

    Selling Bitcoin is taxed differently across Europe: Germany exempts coins held for more than a year, Slovakia taxes every sale. Read the Bitcoin tax guides before you plan your withdrawals.

Questions and answers

How much Bitcoin do I need to retire?

It depends on your yearly spending, how long the money has to last and how fast Bitcoin grows. Retiring today at 50 and spending €30,000 a year until 85, this calculator gives about 6.6 BTC in the conservative scenario (10% a year), 3.4 BTC in the base scenario (20%) and 2.4 BTC in the optimistic one (30%), with Bitcoin at €58,646 on 16 Nov 2026. Enter your own numbers above.

How many Bitcoin do I need to retire?

For spending of €50,000 a year from age 50 to 85, the calculator gives about 5.7 BTC in the base scenario and 10.9 BTC in the conservative one. At €20,000 a year it is 2.3 BTC and 4.4 BTC; at €80,000 a year, 9.1 BTC and 17.5 BTC. These are hypothetical scenarios with Bitcoin at €58,646 on 16 Nov 2026, not forecasts. Retiring later lowers the number, because the stack can grow first.

Is 1 BTC enough to retire?

For most people retiring now, no. With this calculator's assumptions (retire at 50, spend until 85, spending rising 2% a year), 1 BTC at €58,646 covers about €4,600 a year in the conservative scenario, €8,800 in the base scenario and €12,600 in the optimistic one. If you retire in 20 or 30 years and keep buying every month, test 1 BTC in the calculator above.

What is the best Bitcoin retirement calculator?

One that shows its assumptions. Look for a calculator that lets you set your own spending, retirement age, life expectancy, growth and inflation, works in your currency, shows the result year by year and says the scenarios are not forecasts. This one does that in euros, runs in your browser, stores nothing and needs no sign-up. It leaves out tax and market falls, so read the result as a range.

Can I retire on Bitcoin in Europe?

Yes, but the tax on selling depends on where you live. Germany exempts Bitcoin held for more than a year and Czechia after three years, while Slovakia taxes every sale as income and adds health insurance contributions. In the EU, the provider you buy from should hold a MiCA licence, which you can check on the ESMA register. Keep a record of every purchase for your tax return.

What happens to a Bitcoin retirement in a bear market?

You sell more Bitcoin for the same spending, so the stack can run out early. Bitcoin has fallen 70% or more from a high four times since 2011 (2011, 2013–2015, 2017–2019 and 2021–2022, US dollar month-end prices). This calculator assumes steady growth with no falls. If the price never rose at all, spending €30,000 a year from 50 to 85 would need about 26.6 BTC today. A cash reserve lets you avoid selling after a fall.

Is this a forecast?

No. Bitcoin's price is volatile and the growth rates are hypothetical scenarios. Past performance doesn't guarantee future results, and Bitcoin can fall in value.

What does "BTC needed today" mean?

The smallest stack you would need today to retire at your chosen age without saving anything more, under the same growth and inflation assumptions.

Are taxes included?

No. Selling Bitcoin can be taxable in your country. The Bitcoin tax guides explain the rules for 13 EU countries.

Does Virtuse hold my Bitcoin?

No. The calculator runs in your browser and stores nothing. Virtuse never holds your keys or funds.
