## 1.2 SAM — Serviceable Addressable Market (Non-MiCA)

### MiCA Exclusions: The Regulatory Safe Harbor

- **Recital 83** explicitly states: "hardware or software providers of non-custodial wallets should not fall within the scope of this regulation" [^31^].
- **Articles 68–71** confirm that non-custodial wallet holders themselves are NOT subject to MiCA obligations [^26^].
- **P2P transactions** without intermediaries fall outside MiCA's CASP definition entirely [^32^].
- **Fully decentralized protocols** (Bisq, RoboSats), **self-hosted payment processors** (BTCPay Server), and **Bitcoin mining** are not CASP activities [^36^].
- The AMLR explicitly excludes wallet software providers that do not have access to or control over users' crypto assets [^37^].

| Service Category | MiCA Status | Legal Basis |
|-----------------|-------------|-------------|
| Non-custodial hardware wallets | **EXCLUDED** | Recital 83 [^31^] |
| Non-custodial software wallets | **EXCLUDED** | Recital 83, Art 68–71 [^26^] |
| P2P Bitcoin trading (no intermediary) | **EXCLUDED** | No CASP definition met [^32^] |
| Fully decentralized exchanges (Bisq) | **EXCLUDED** | "Fully decentralized" exemption |
| Lightning Network (non-custodial) | **EXCLUDED** | Decentralized infrastructure [^13^] |
| Self-hosted payment processors (BTCPay) | **EXCLUDED** | No custody, no CASP [^36^] |
| Bitcoin mining (self-mining) | **NOT REGULATED** | Not a CASP activity |

### Key Structural Insights

1. **Non-custodial wallets dominate SAM** ($1.06B combined hardware + software = ~70% of total SAM) [^4^] — driven by European privacy consciousness and post-MiCA self-custody migration.
2. **MiCA's exclusionary framework is a feature, not a bug** — it creates competitive moats for services that structurally cannot be regulated as CASPs [^31^][^26^].

### 2.1 Trend 1: Regulatory Arbitrage Post-MiCA

**Description.** MiCA's explicit exclusion of "hardware or software providers of non-custodial wallets" from its scope [^529^] creates a regulatory safe harbor that is accelerating demand migration from custodial to self-custody services. As of early 2025, over 50 CASPs had lost their licenses due to MiCA non-compliance [^35^] [^76^], while only 12 CASPs and 10 EMT issuers had obtained licenses out of 3,000+ registered VASPs [^35^]. This custodial-platform contraction is funneling users toward Bitcoin-only, non-custodial alternatives that operate entirely outside MiCA's licensing framework.

**European-Specific Evidence.**

- Europe commands 30% of global hardware wallet demand — second-largest market globally [^37^]. Germany, France, and the Netherlands led sales growth, driven by favorable fintech regulations and high crypto literacy [^37^].
- Relai (Switzerland) became the first Bitcoin-only startup to secure MiCA authorization in 2025, reporting 300% YoY user growth and 75,000+ registered users, with projected expansion from 8 million to 450 million addressable market [^575^] [^577^] [^578^].
- The hardware wallet market was valued at $680 million globally in 2025, projected to reach $4.77 billion by 2035 at 21% CAGR [^3^]. Ledger (France) and Trezor (Czech Republic) together control 62% of the global hardware wallet market [^158^].
- Hardware and desktop wallets together represent 39% of regional wallet usage in Europe — significantly higher than the 31% global average [^46^].

This trend directly benefits Bitcoin-only hardware wallets, multisig solutions, and open-source wallet software.

### 4.4.4 Key Capital Flow Insights

1. **France dominates by volume, Switzerland by conviction.** French companies (Ledger, Bitstack, ACINQ, LN Markets) attracted ~$700M+ in total VC; Swiss firms (Relai, Tangem, FUTURE) command the highest share of Bitcoin-only fund capital [^582^][^686^].

2. **Hardware wallets are the revenue kings but software is the growth engine.** Ledger's $70.9M dwarfs all app-based revenue, yet Bitstack's 10× growth in 2 years and Relai's 300% YoY growth signal where capital will flow next [^59^][^527^][^686^].

3. **Grant funding is maturing as a parallel capital stack.** OpenSats alone deploys $12M+ annually — creating a non-dilutive funding layer for Bitcoin infrastructure that no other crypto segment can match [^536^].

4. **The Bitcoin-only VC category is emerging as distinct from general crypto VC.** Ego Death Capital's $100M Fund II and TVP's institutional LP base demonstrate that Bitcoin-native investment is becoming an institutional asset class, not a niche [^582^][^608^].

5. **B2B infrastructure represents the largest uncaptured opportunity.** Europe dominates Bitcoin hardware (60%+ global share) and node infrastructure (36.7% of nodes), yet has no equivalent to US-based Lightspark or Voltage in the Lightning infrastructure layer.

6. **Ledger's projected $4B IPO is a market-validating event.** If executed, it would represent the largest European crypto-native public listing and establish a benchmark for Bitcoin hardware valuations globally [^512^].
