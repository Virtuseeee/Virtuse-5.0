Your exchange is reporting your Bitcoin trades: DAC8 in Austria

Since 1 January 2026, crypto exchanges record the identity, tax ID and trades of Austrian residents under DAC8. Austrian providers report 2026 data by 31 July 2027. Gains on Bitcoin bought after February 2021 are taxed at 27.5% however long you hold it; only coins bought earlier and held over a year are tax-free.

What DAC8 is

DAC8 (EU Directive 2023/2226) brings crypto into the automatic exchange of tax information that already covers bank accounts. Austria implemented it on time with the Crypto Reporting Obligation Act (Krypto-Meldepflichtgesetz, BGBl. I No. 96/2025), in force since 1 January 2026.

Providers reporting in Austria send the data to the Federal Ministry of Finance. Exchanges licensed in other EU countries report to their own tax authority, which sends the data to Austria.

What your exchange reports

ReportedDetails
Who you areName, address, date of birth and tax identification number
Crypto to moneyTotal amounts bought and sold per crypto-asset and year
Crypto to cryptoTotal amounts per crypto-asset
TransfersTransfers out of the exchange, including to your own wallet

Moving Bitcoin between your own wallets is not taxed, but a transfer out of an exchange shows up in the report. Keep a record that the address is yours.

If you used an Austrian provider, it usually withheld the tax already. Gains through foreign exchanges are your job to declare, and that data arrives after you file.

How Bitcoin is taxed in Austria

  • Crypto bought after 28 February 2021 is taxed at the special rate of 27.5%, however long you held it. The rate does not raise the tax on your other income.
  • Crypto bought before 1 March 2021 follows the old rules: a sale after more than one year is tax-free.
  • Swapping one crypto-asset for another is tax-neutral: the purchase cost carries over to the new coin.
  • Austrian crypto providers withhold the 27.5% tax (KESt) on sales, which settles it. Gains through foreign exchanges must be declared in your annual return.
  • Lending and mining income is taxed when received. Staking rewards, airdrops and hard forks are not taxed when received, but count with a purchase cost of zero when you sell.
  • Transaction fees reduce the gain. Losses can offset other capital income in the same year.

What to do now

  1. Export your full trade history from every exchange you have used, including closed accounts.
  2. Separate coins bought before March 2021 from later purchases; only the older ones can be tax-free.
  3. Declare gains through foreign exchanges in your 2026 return.
  4. If you did not declare gains in earlier years, talk to a tax advisor about a voluntary disclosure (Selbstanzeige) before the tax office acts.
  5. When you withdraw to your own wallet, keep proof that the address belongs to you.

FAQ

Is Bitcoin tax-free after one year in Austria?

Only if you bought it before 1 March 2021. Bitcoin bought later is taxed at 27.5% whenever you sell.

Do I pay tax when I swap Bitcoin for another crypto-asset?

No. A crypto-to-crypto swap is tax-neutral in Austria; the purchase cost moves to the new coin.

Is my hardware wallet reported?

No. Your wallet's balance is not reported, but transfers from an exchange to it are.

My Austrian exchange already withheld tax. Do I still declare?

No. Withholding by an Austrian provider settles the tax. Gains through foreign exchanges still go in your return.
