DAC8 in Spain: your Bitcoin trades are being recorded

Spain has not yet passed its DAC8 law, and the European Commission opened proceedings against it in January 2026. Exchanges in other EU countries already record Spanish residents' trades and must send them to the AEAT by 30 September 2027. Spain taxes crypto gains, including crypto-to-crypto swaps, at 19% to 30%.

What DAC8 is

DAC8 (EU Directive 2023/2226) brings crypto into the automatic exchange of tax information that already covers bank accounts. Member states had to adopt it by the end of 2025.

Spain missed that deadline. A bill amending the General Tax Law to add DAC8 has not been published in the official gazette, and in January 2026 the European Commission sent Spain a letter of formal notice. Draft regulations treat 2026 as the first reporting year, with a new form 175 for providers replacing form 173.

Exchanges licensed in other EU countries record Spanish customers under their own countries' laws and report to their own tax authority, which sends the data to the Spanish Tax Agency (AEAT).

Moving Bitcoin between your own wallets is not taxed, but a transfer out of an exchange shows up in the report. Keep a record that the address is yours.

The AEAT already receives data on crypto balances held with Spanish providers, and its 2026 control plan focuses on crypto.

How Bitcoin is taxed in Spain

  • Gains from selling crypto are capital gains in the savings base of the IRPF.
  • Savings income is taxed at 19% up to €6,000, 21% up to €50,000, 23% up to €200,000, 27% up to €300,000 and 30% above.
  • Swapping one crypto-asset for another is a taxable exchange, so a gain or loss arises on every swap.
  • The tax authority applies FIFO: the first coins you bought count as the first sold.
  • There is no holding-period exemption.
  • A net loss offsets other savings-base gains, and up to 25% of savings income; the rest carries forward for four years.
  • If crypto you hold with providers outside Spain is worth over €50,000 on 31 December, you must file form 721 between 1 January and 31 March.

What to do now

  1. Export your full trade history from every exchange you have used, including closed accounts.
  2. Calculate every sale and every crypto-to-crypto swap using FIFO.
  3. Check whether your holdings abroad were over €50,000 on 31 December 2026 and need form 721.
  4. If you left out gains in earlier years, talk to a tax advisor about a supplementary return (declaración complementaria). Filing before the AEAT contacts you avoids penalties, though surcharges apply.
  5. When you withdraw to your own wallet, keep proof that the address belongs to you.

FAQ

Spain has no DAC8 law yet. Am I reported?

Yes, by exchanges in other EU countries, which record your 2026 trades and must send them to Spain by 30 September 2027.

Do I pay tax when I swap Bitcoin for another crypto-asset?

Yes. In Spain a crypto-to-crypto swap is a taxable exchange, so the gain or loss counts in that year.

Is my hardware wallet reported?

No. Your wallet's balance is not reported, but transfers from an exchange to it are.

How far back can the AEAT check?

Generally four years from the end of the filing period for each year.
