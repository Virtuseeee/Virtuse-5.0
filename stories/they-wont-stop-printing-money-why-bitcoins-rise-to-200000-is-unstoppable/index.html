“They Won’t Stop Printing Money!” Why Bitcoin’s Rise to $200,000 Is Unstoppable

By Ras Vasilisin Macro desk 20 Aug 2026 · 12:12 UTC Block — 4 min
Share

A confession: those two days caught me off guard too

Bitcoin rocketed upward over the last 48 hours. 

My first reaction wasn’t excitement. It was doubt. I opened the news. I read the same lines you probably did. “Technical bounce.” “Speculative buying.” “Short term euphoria.” 

Explanations that sound reasonable for thirty seconds. Then they fall apart the moment you think about them for one more minute.

I’m not new to this market. But I’m not some untouchable expert either. 

For years I thought I could track the Fed closely enough, watch their statements and their “hawkish” rate signals, and predict where the market would move. 

I was wrong. 

That’s exactly why one piece of macro analysis stopped me in my tracks a few days ago. Not because it promised quick riches. Because instead of another dose of media noise, it offered something far more uncomfortable. Plain math.

That math says something simple. It doesn’t matter what reasons mainstream media feed you day after day. 

The reality is different. The system has a debt problem. Rhetoric can’t fix it. It can only be delayed by printing money.

Here’s the argument. Step by step. The same way I had to work through it myself.

Step 1: The numbers you need to remember. Not hype. An 18 month forecast

Macro investor Larry Lepard doesn’t offer a vague “bitcoin goes up.” He gives a specific timeframe and specific numbers. Gold at $7,000 per troy ounce. Silver at $200 per troy ounce. Bitcoin at $200,000 per BTC.

“This isn’t happening tomorrow. But I’m very confident it happens within 18 months. These numbers aren’t pulled from thin air. They’re the simple mathematical result of an unsustainable debt trajectory and constant monetary expansion.”

Larry Lepard

The key word is “mathematical.” This isn’t a bet on market mood. It’s the consequence of what’s happening behind the scenes. That’s worth breaking down.

Step 2: Why the money supply has to grow

The first reason is simple. 

The fiat system has a built in flaw. Debt and money supply have to move together. When one falls behind, the other has to catch up. US national debt has already crossed $40 trillion. 

$3.5 trillion of that was added in just the last year and a half. To keep paying principal and rising interest, the total money supply has to keep growing. The Fed has tried to hold a tight line since 2022. But that stance can’t hold forever without something breaking in the system.

Step 3: Why the market is reading this wrong

The second reason is the myth of a “hawkish” Fed. 

Markets believe the new Fed chief will get tough, raise rates, and tame inflation by force. 

The truth is the opposite. 

The new Fed chief isn’t counting on higher rates to fix inflation. Lower rates are meant to cheapen investment, boost the supply of goods and services, and cool inflation from below instead. The plan isn’t to stop inflation. It’s to let the American economy run hot and simply grow its way out of debt. In other words, while the market waits for a hawkish pivot, the new Fed chief is planning the exact opposite.

Step 4: Why Europe isn’t a safe harbor

The third reason is that this isn’t just a US story. 

The ECB has historically copied whatever the Fed does. J

ust with a slight delay and a weaker economic base. The dollar makes up 85 to 88 percent of all foreign exchange transactions worldwide. So when the Fed debases the dollar, the ECB is forced to respond the same way. 

Otherwise a sharply stronger euro would crush European exports. With a heavily indebted southern eurozone, the EU has even less room than the American economy to keep rates high. If the debt bubble bursts across the Atlantic, Europe fires up its own printing presses right behind it.

Step 5: Where the capital flows

The fourth and final reason is where capital moves once the market realizes currency debasement is permanent, not temporary. 

Gold and silver, as analog hard money with a thousand year track record, usually sense and reflect liquidity shifts first. 

Bitcoin, as digital hard money, works as the cleanest indicator of global liquidity. During tight policy it can look calm, even frustratingly cheap. But it reacts to monetary stimulus with a lag. And once the printers start running again, its move is the sharpest of them all.

TL;DR. 

It doesn’t matter what the next anchor with a polished smile tells you tonight. 

Bitcoin goes up because politicians printed money, are printing money, and will keep printing money. That’s the one thing you can rely on one hundred percent, with more certainty than any weather forecast. 

At least in that, the system is remarkably reliable.
