November Isn’t Bitcoin’s Best Month. One Year Did the Work.

By Ras Vasilisin Markets desk 1 Nov 2026 · 14:12 UTC Block — 3 min
Share

No. Not the way the headline number makes it sound.

November’s average return since 2010 is +34.4%. Highest of any month. Most of that comes from one year. In November 2013, Bitcoin did +451%.

Switch to the median and November falls to +8.5%. That puts it fourth out of twelve. Fine, but nothing special.

By median, February is the best month at +11.7%. October held the crown until last night. August is the worst at −8.4%. September, the one everyone loves to hate, has closed higher four years straight.

Why the average lies

An average adds up every year and divides by the count. One crazy year can carry the whole thing.

Bitcoin has had 16 full Novembers. Fifteen of them landed between −37% and +56%. Then there’s 2013, at +451%.

Take that one year out and the November average drops from +34.4% to about +7%. The median barely moves.

The median is just the middle year. Half the Novembers did better. Half did worse. If you want to know what a normal November looks like, that’s the number.

To be fair, November’s record is decent. It closed higher in 10 of 16 years, or 63%. The average month manages 57%. Good. Not magic.

Look at the gap between each bar and its circle. Where the gap is wide (April, October, November), a few monster years are doing all the work.

September, the “worst month”

September earned its reputation. Lowest average of any month, −2.7%. Closed higher in only 8 of 17 years.

Then it stopped cooperating. Up 3.9% in 2023, 7.3% in 2024, 5.2% in 2025 and 6.4% in 2026.

By median, the real laggard is August at −8.4%. It fell four years straight, from 2022 to 2025. Then August 2026 went up 24.9%.

October is doing the opposite. The “best month” closed down 3.9% last year. This year it closed down 7.4%.

That cost it first place. October’s median slipped from +13.0% to +11.2%, and February (+11.7%) moved to the top.

That’s calendar seasonality in Bitcoin. It describes the past nicely and the next year badly. Somewhere, a thread called “BTC seasonality just got humbled” is already live.

Sixteen years is a tiny sample

Each month has 16 or 17 data points. That’s it.

One weird year can drag an average a long way. November 2013 proves it. A short losing streak can make a month look cursed, like August until this summer.

The early years make it worse. 2011 alone holds the best result for six of the twelve months, and the worst for four. Bitcoin was tiny back then. A 50% month barely made the news.

So a month’s record tells you as much about which years landed in it as about the calendar. You can’t build a trading rule on that.

What to do with it

Treat seasonality as context, not a timing plan. A good November this year won’t prove the pattern. A bad one won’t disprove it. Sixteen years can’t settle it either way.

The decisions that actually change your outcome are slower and more boring. How much you buy. How often. What it costs you. Where the coins end up.
