Follow the Money: Why I Think Binance Is Behind This Crash

Bitcoin fell 13% in a week and everyone blames the Fed. I follow the money from Binance’s $6.3 billion hack and explain why I think it’s behind the crash.

By Ras Vasilisin Markets desk 15 Nov 2026 · 21:51 UTC Block — 3 min
Share

Bitcoin lost 13% this week. It closed last Sunday at $74,743, touched $61,947 today, its lowest price since July, and sits around $65,000 tonight. It’s down about 26% for the year.

Everyone’s blaming the Fed, the dollar and the ETFs. I think it’s pretty obvious who’s really behind this. Binance.

The motive

On 29 October, North Korean hackers took about $6.3 billion out of Binance in roughly 20 minutes. It’s the biggest crypto theft ever. On 12 November Binance said every customer had been made whole. It put in more than $6.34 billion of its own money and bought the missing coins “through market and over-the-counter channels.”

So ask yourself one simple question. If you had to buy back billions of dollars of crypto, would you rather pay $76,000 per bitcoin or $63,000?

The pattern

Ever since the hack, traders have watched the same movie on repeat. Price gets shoved into a pile of leveraged longs, the longs get liquidated, somebody quietly buys the cheap coins, and then it starts again. Last Sunday night, 8 November, about $1 billion of positions got wiped out in ten minutes. On Friday the day’s liquidations passed $1.6 billion. And through all of it, Binance’s own data showed about 3.2 accounts long for every one short. The crowd kept walking into the same trap.

This isn’t the first time Binance’s market makers have been in the spotlight. In 2023 the SEC said a CZ-owned firm, Sigma Chain, wash-traded on Binance.US, and that billions in customer money went to Merit Peak, another CZ company. The case was dropped in 2025 without anyone being found liable. But the setup hasn’t changed: the biggest exchange, market makers close to it, and order books nobody outside can see.

Can I prove it? No. Nobody can, yet. But motive, means and pattern all point the same way.

The rest just piled on

The ETFs didn’t help. US spot Bitcoin funds lost $1.07 billion last week and $2.35 billion over two weeks, seven red days in a row. A strong dollar and a hot inflation print did the rest. Funds and copy-traders don’t care why a chart breaks. They just sell when it does.

Fear at 10, Bitcoin on sale

The Fear & Greed Index ended the week at 10. Since 2018 it’s been that low on only about 2% of days. The last time was June, a few weeks before Bitcoin bottomed at $57,700 and ran back to $87,000.

Price is now just under the line I watch most, the 200-week moving average, at about $68,000. Every major low, in 2018, 2020 and 2022, came at or below it. Since 2015, every week Bitcoin closed within 5% of that line, it was higher a year later. Every single one. The median gain was over 100%.

It dipped under that line in June and August too. Buying early hurt for a few weeks. It didn’t hurt for long.

Binance shook the tree

Somebody needed cheap coins this month, and I think we all know who. Leveraged traders handed them over. If your bitcoin sits on an exchange, you’re playing at their table, with their order book and their liquidation engine. Take it home. Then let Binance shake the tree as hard as it likes. Your coins won’t fall out.
