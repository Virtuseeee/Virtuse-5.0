Don’t Fall for the “AI Danger” Scare

AI isn’t the threat. The people selling the rescue are.

By Ras Vasilisin Policy desk 21 Sept 2026 · 08:56 UTC Block — 2 min
Share

The Pattern of Panic

A few months ago, each new AI drop hit a nerve. What if they had finally gone too far?

I read the panic, sat with the extinction pieces, and bought it for a stretch. The end of the world had been renamed: the algorithm.

Then the pattern emerged. First it was global warming. Then the virus. Now AI.

Every time the end-of-the-world choir starts, the exact same offer arrives with it: We will protect you. Just hand over the controls.

The Oldest Trick in the Book

This isn’t a claim that AI cannot cause harm. It can.

This is a warning about the oldest trick in the book: They talk Armageddon, then they sell the cage.

That is First Thessalonians 5:3 in action: “For when they shall say, Peace and safety; then sudden destruction cometh upon them.” When they preach peace and safety, sudden ruin follows.

Safety is the slogan, but your freedom is the fee.

A small priesthood of “better people” decides everyone else is too dumb, too dangerous, or too free to run their own tools.

Full Throttle for Them, Speed Limits for You

Over the weekend, the biggest names in AI were practically begging the industry to pump the brakes. Anthropic put out their “pace yourselves” memo, a staffer dramatically resigned over existential threats, and think tanks had the outrage machine spun up within minutes.

But take a look at what’s actually happening behind the scenes. They’re not freezing hiring, slashing capital expenditure, or shelving their next massive training run.

In other words: full throttle for them, speed limits for everyone else.

The War on Open Source and Decentralization

What they really want is a federal overseer packed with their people, legal cover to coordinate, and rules that freeze open source. Open models are to these labs what Bitcoin was to central banks.

You don’t have to beat them on a leaderboard; you only have to get good enough to run code on your own hardware. Once you do, nobody gets to rent you their meter.

Decentralization must be framed as reckless so they can protect you from it.

Watch the sequence. Climate became the excuse to control heavy industry. The pandemic became the excuse to control healthcare. AI is becoming the excuse to control computers.

The Real Risks and Global Competition

If free nations and open-source developers take their foot off the gas out of moral panic, markets do not win. China does not pause as a courtesy, and the labs begging Washington for a leash won’t pause either.

Real risks like fraud and weaponized code are engineering and crime problems. Laundering them into a total ban on open systems doesn’t buy safety—it buys a central off-switch, with someone else’s hand on it.

Take Control

Build. Own your own intelligence. Run models you control on hardware you hold.

Do not outsource the brain of your company to a lab that begs for regulation over the weekend and raises its next billion on Monday.

Kneel to God only.

Not to the machine, and certainly not to the committee that swears it will save you from it.
