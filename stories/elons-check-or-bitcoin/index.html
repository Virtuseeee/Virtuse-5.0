Elon’s Check or Bitcoin

By Ras Vasilisin Policy desk 19 Oct 2026 · 08:28 UTC Block — 3 min
Share

On Thursday, October 15, a Tesla Roadster drove up a ramp upside down, bounced off, used cold-gas thrusters to leap over five other Teslas and landed softly. It hit 97 km/h in 0.81 seconds.

In June 2018, Elon Musk wrote that the Roadster would get a “SpaceX option package” with about ten small rocket thrusters. “Maybe they will even allow a Tesla to fly.” People laughed. They thought he was joking.

He wasn’t.

Almost everything

In 2006, Musk published his “secret plan”: build an expensive sports car, use the money to build a cheaper one, then a mass-market one. In 2023, the Tesla Model Y was the best-selling car in the world, with 1.22 million units. It was the first electric car in history to top the global ranking.

Landing a rocket was supposedly impossible too. In December 2015, a Falcon 9 brought its first stage back to the ground for the first time. Today, a single booster has flown 37 times.

Why do I say “almost”? Because Musk regularly gets the calendar wrong. The Roadster was supposed to be on the road in 2020, and he has been promising robotaxis “next year” for years.

Elon gets the dates wrong. Not the direction.

A prediction that is already coming true

In November 2016, he told CNBC: “There is a pretty good chance we end up with a universal basic income, or something like that, due to automation.”

Three months later, at the summit in Dubai, he added: “There will be fewer and fewer jobs that a robot cannot do better. These are not things that I wish will happen. These are simply things that I think will probably happen.”

Back then it sounded like science fiction. Today it’s a spreadsheet.

In February, Jack Dorsey’s Block laid off more than 4,000 people, almost half the company, and the stock jumped as much as 24%. According to Challenger, Gray & Christmas, US companies announced 120,136 job cuts from January to September that they attributed to AI. That makes AI the most common reason for layoffs this year.

The Tesla Semi and the Cybercab can drive without a driver. Truck drivers and taxi drivers hold exactly the kind of jobs the middle class is built on.

Andrew Yang, one of the biggest advocates of UBI, said in March: “We have a brutal one-to-three-year window where we need a universal basic income as a bridge.”

The window is open. Now.

UBI or not

Musk’s answer is clear.

In April, he wrote: “Universal HIGH INCOME via checks issued by the Federal government is the best way to deal with unemployment caused by AI. AI/robotics will produce goods & services far in excess of the increase in the money supply, so there will not be inflation.” (In other words, an income from the state that everyone gets, with no conditions attached.)

Income from the government is not wealth produced by a robot. It’s new dollars. The government either borrows them or prints them.

And who explained this best?

Musk himself, in October 2025: “That is why Bitcoin is based on energy: you can issue fake fiat currency, and every government in history has done so, but it is impossible to fake energy.”

Every government in history. His words.

Technology is deflationary. If robots make life cheaper while the government prints checks, you don’t get that gift. It dissolves into new money, and everyone who saves in euros or dollars pays for it.

Why Bitcoin

Because it’s the only thing that fits Musk’s own logic. In November 2025 he said: “Energy is the true currency. This is why I said Bitcoin is based on energy. You can’t legislate energy.”

The network’s hashrate is around 1,040 EH/s, and on Friday mining difficulty rose by 5.9%. Every block is backed by real energy. Nobody can print it.

There will only ever be 21 million bitcoin. No parliament and no AI agent can change that.

AI can do your job. It can’t create another bitcoin.

Your decision

You won’t decide on UBI alone. But what you save in when it arrives is your decision alone.

If Elon is right, and he usually is, a sword of Damocles hangs over the middle class.

You can wait for a handout from the state. Or you can save in money nobody can print.

The choice is ours.
