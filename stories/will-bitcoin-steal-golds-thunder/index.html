Will Bitcoin steal Gold’s Thunder?

By Ras Vasilisin Macro desk 8 Jan 2021 · 13:15 UTC Block — 3 min
Share

In case you missed it, the hottest debate on Wall Street is whether Bitcoin will rival gold as an inflation hedge.

JPMorgan analysts highlighted data recently that suggests that investors are beginning to ditch gold for Bitcoin.

We’re seeing something interesting right now.

Bitcoin is eating the world, and it’s the most dominant asset right now. 

Is Bitcoin stealing gold’s thunder?

Let’s first evaluate the most significant pros and cons of the Bitcoin vs. gold debate, starting with Bitcoin’s benefits as a “digital gold.”

Pros

Easily sold

Because Bitcoin is entirely digital, it can be easily transferred to another user and sold for its dollar or euro value. You can send your Bitcoin in person using a QR code, or you can use an exchange service that will digitally connect you with a buyer. As BlackRock’s chief investment officer Rick Rieder told CNBC in a recent interview, trading Bitcoin “is so much more functional than passing a bar of gold around.”

Demographics

The world has changed. You and I are in different places on the planet, but you can read this article instantly. Gold used to be the only answer for the alternative financial system for centuries. But we live in the digital age now. The younger generation is digitally native. They want to store, transact, and use digital goods and assets. The demographics are clearly on the side of Bitcoin.

Scarcity is key

Scarcity is a big part of the picture. While gold is narratively scarce, but Bitcoin is scarce provably. There is a widespread belief that there is a little supply of gold. U.S. geological survey estimates that there are around 57,000 tons of gold left to be mined. But their estimate considers only proven reserves. The probable or yet undiscovered reserves are unknown.

However, in Bitcoin, you can prove the exact supply. There will only ever be 21 million Bitcoin. There are only 900 Bitcoin produced per day at the moment. You can verify it on the actual blockchain, which is immutable. The scarcity in Bitcoin is programmatic, transparent, and provable. Make no mistake, Bitcoin’s scarcity is superior to gold’s.

Now little cheerleading for Bitcoin isn’t necessarily a bad thing. But unlike your inner circle, most people are still scared of Bitcoin. So, here are my two cents on the disadvantage of using Bitcoin as a replacement for gold.

Cons

Gold’s 5,000-year history

The primary factor in favor of gold is that it has an established record of 5,000 years. Some gold proponents argue that Bitcoin shouldn’t even exist in the same sentence with gold. For hundreds of years, gold has dominated the safe-haven asset arena. Bitcoin is only over a decade old. For better or worse, gold is likely to stick around for many more generations.

Practical uses

Gold has dual characteristics.  It is a safe-haven investment and a luxury good. 50% of gold demand comes from jewelry. It is used in many other applications such as dentistry and electronics. Gold is the king of precious metals, and it has clearly defined uses and demand, even without being a safe-haven investment.

Stable investment

Gold is an 11 trillion dollar asset, while the Bitcoin market cap is about 350 billion. Hence, it’s less volatile and more accepted than Bitcoin by investors. It has many attributes to be a stable asset. The value of gold remained high even during the government’s attempts to outlaw it. Similarly, gold is an inert metal that never corrodes and lasts forever.  Gold has all the ingredients to survive the next apocalypse. 

Conclusion

Both Bitcoin and gold offer solutions to the problem of excessive money printing.

They both work for different people at different times; they complement each other, and they will all co-exist together for a long time. 

Even though Bitcoin might not steal gold’s thunder entirely, it’s interesting enough to own some of both.
