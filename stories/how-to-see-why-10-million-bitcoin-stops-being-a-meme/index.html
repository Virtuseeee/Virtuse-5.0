How to See Why $10 Million Bitcoin Stops Being a Meme

By Ras Vasilisin Macro desk 28 Sept 2026 · 11:00 UTC Block — 2 min
Share

I still flinch when the chart goes quiet.

$80,000 looks like a ceiling when you check it twice a day. I used to treat every flat week as proof I had overread the story. I am not a natural holder. I talk myself out of the long case, then talk myself back in. The moment of truth was boring.

I stopped asking when the next candle prints and asked what has to be true for one coin to be worth ten million.

Three answers survived that question. None of them need a hero call for next week.

First, watch the measuring stick break, not the ticker

Governments keep spending. Debt keeps climbing. Fiat survives if the currency takes the hit.

When trust in the dollar slips, people do not wait for a committee. They look for an asset nobody can print.

Bitcoin then stops acting like a risk ticker and starts acting like the last clean unit of account. The dollar price can look insane while purchasing power is only being restated.

No Bitcoin price is too high against a unit diluted on purpose. The release valve is the currency. Bitcoin is the thing that does not bend.

Second, follow wealth out of the leaking vaults

Gold works. Bonds used to work. Real estate still works for some. All of them leak in slow motion.

Holders notice first. Institutions notice later.

Then the money moves.

Bitcoin is easier to store, easier to send, and impossible to water down.

Once that flow starts, it does not reverse for fashion. Hard assets already sit in the tens of trillions. Even a modest share of that pile lifts one coin into the millions. The migration does not need a panic headline every day. It needs years of people choosing the harder money.

Third, price digital gold the way Amazon repriced retail

The internet did not copy bookstores.

It made Amazon larger than anyone in the old catalog business thought possible.

Access exploded.

Inventory went global. The addressable market was not the shop on the corner. It was everyone with a connection.

The same pattern hits stores of value. Gold served the analog world of vaults and borders. Bitcoin serves phones. More people can hold it than ever held physical gold. It splits into tiny units. It crosses a border without a truck. Institutions are already arriving.

Sovereigns will arrive after them. Retail in places where gold is hard to buy is already there.

Scarcity plus global access is how five to ten million becomes a base case instead of a slogan. The market is not gold copied onto a screen. The market is gold plus the internet.

Then hold one thesis, not three bets

Broken money pushes demand.

Old wealth follows the better rail. The internet multiplies who can own the rail.

Twenty one million coins do not grow. The world around them does. Time does the compounding. Stack for that world, not for the next candle.

TL;DR

Ten million is what happens when a fixed pile of coins prices a larger, weaker-money world. Stop arguing the week. Argue the market that can actually form.
