Bitcoin Beat the War. November Still Gets a Vote.

Up 65% against gold since the February low and ahead of every major asset since the war began. October has the history to extend the lead. November has the history to take it back.

By Ras Vasilisin Macro desk 4 Oct 2026 · 21:22 UTC Block — 3 min
Share

Bitcoin finished the week at $84,638, up 0.8%.

That is the whole dollar story. A failed push toward $87,000, a tanker strike in the Strait of Hormuz, and a slide back into the mid $83,000s. Oil barely noticed. Bitcoin, which never closes, did.

The thesis is simpler than the tape. This market is no longer being settled in dollars. It is being settled against gold, against stocks, and against a war that started in late February. On that board Bitcoin is already ahead. October has the history to extend the lead. November has the history to take it back.

The war scoreboard

Since the Iran war began, Bitcoin is the best performing major asset on the board that matters. About 24% in dollars. The Nasdaq 100 is up about 22%. The S&P 500 about 11%. Inflation linked bonds are down around 7%. Gold is down about 21%. Silver closer to 35%.

A risk asset was supposed to sell off when the missiles flew. It led the tape instead.

Sixty five percent against gold

The cleaner number is the ratio. Bitcoin is up 65% against gold since the February low.

One coin bought about 12 ounces of gold at the February low. It now buys about 20. Gold has given back a fifth from the war spike. Bitcoin has ground higher. Relative performance did the work the dollar price has not.

It has not yet had to reprice a full debasement of the dollar. It has only had to outrun gold during a shooting war.

Uptober, on the record

October is Bitcoin’s best month on the monthly returns heatmap. Since 2013 only three Octobers have closed red. Average gain about 18%, median about 13%, ten up years against three down. The red ones were shallow.

Strength has tended to build in the middle and later weeks, not on day one. Whale stablecoin inflows to Binance are up 40% over thirty days, to about $30 billion. Cash parked on the exchange. Usually a prelude, not a conclusion.

The seasonal base rate is real. It is not a schedule.

The November cut

The risk is not October. It is the vote.

US midterms fall on 3 November. The last three midterm fourth quarters were all red, on CoinGlass quarterly data, even in years when October itself was flat or green.

The sample is small, and it shares a clock with the halving. Small is not the same as empty. In 2018 and 2022 the damage landed in November, not in Uptober.

The map is specific. Lose $82,000 and the short term holder cost basis near $73,000 is the level that matters. Hold $82,000 and the medium term path still runs toward $100,000.

The bond market underneath

The fiscal tape got louder in the same week. President Trump said certain levels of inflation would pay the debt down very rapidly, and that he wants rates at 1% or less. The same day the 10 year touched 5.34%, the highest since 2002, and closed at 5.24%. Interest on the debt is already near $1 trillion a year. Buybacks were tripled to $6 billion. Gross issuance is more than $2 trillion. The long end of the Treasury market has been described as totally bidless.

A bidless bond and an open talk of inflating the debt away are the macro version of the gold chart. They do not set next week’s price. They explain why a 0.8% week can still be the start of a rotation.

October can press the lead. The election month is the only calendar that has cut this kind of lead before.

The dollar chart is asleep. The gold chart is not. That gap is the trade. November is the date it gets tested.
