Europe’s online gambling machine hit €128 billion. Somehow Bitcoin is still the thing you get warned about

By Ras Vasilisin Policy desk 25 Oct 2026 · 15:58 UTC Block — 4 min
Share

People hear I own a Bitcoin company and the reply comes fast.

It’s gambling.

For years I answered with data. Just holding Bitcoin beats nearly every other asset. I thought logic would win.

One night my nephew sat on the couch with his phone.

He topped up a betting app with another few hundred. Said nothing about it.

No research. No thesis. Nothing you could call investing. Just a casino glow on the screen.

I’d been arguing the wrong fight for years.

The same papers, regulators and governments that warn about Bitcoin’s volatility look fine with online gambling. Prediction markets too.

Fine is generous. Some of them cheer.

Selective protection

Europe wrote MiCA to “protect investors” from crypto. The little guy. Ordinary people. All of that.

Online betting and prediction markets drain young men with ugly consistency. They barely get touched.

Ads run on TV. Governments take the tax.

Gaming Compliance International puts the EU-27 online gambling market at €128 billion in 2025. Unregulated operators took €91.6 billion. That’s 72 percent.

Eastern Europe runs 81 percent unregulated. The same report says governments lost about €22 billion in taxes.

It’s a giant money machine. Prediction markets are just the shiny new model.

Guess who the customer is

Young men. Obviously.

The UK Gambling Commission finds roughly one in ten gamblers aged 18 to 24 scores as a problem gambler. Over 75? Fewer than one in a hundred.

Men land there about twice as often as women.

Male students, per Ygam and GAMSTOP, spend nearly as much on betting each week as on food.

On food.

The ads never stop. Regulators mostly stay quiet.

When Brussels spoke, it shrugged. An MEP asked about the harm. In February 2020, Commissioner Thierry Breton answered for the Commission:

“The Commission is aware of the serious health and social consequences of gambling. However, due to significant differences between Member States in terms of culture, traditions, and attitudes toward various forms of gambling, there are currently no grounds for proposing sector-specific legislation.”

Crypto gets one rulebook for the whole EU. Gambling gets “cultural differences.”

Sure.

The US isn’t cleaner. Americans legally bet a record $166.9 billion on sports in 2025. Sportsbooks kept $17 billion. That was up 23% in a year. States collected $3.7 billion in taxes.

Pew Research says 31% of Americans aged 18 to 29 bet on sports in the past year. For people over 65, it was 12%.

Want to see who pays? Check the helpline. In 2025, nearly half of contacts to the US National Problem Gambling Helpline were aged 18 to 34. 70% were men. 73% cited money trouble.

Europe looks the same.

Dutch adults aged 18 to 23 are 9.3% of the population. They hold 22% of accounts at licensed gambling sites.

In Sweden, about 134,500 people had locked themselves out through the national self-exclusion register by the end of 2025.

And the state’s response? It collects.

US states took a record $18.1 billion in gaming taxes in 2025.

The Dutch regulator‘s own data shows stricter ads for young adults barely move the needle. An earlier report put the illegal market at €617 million in six months. Bigger than the legal one.

Bitcoin in the EU gets MiCA, licences and a drip of warnings. An app that offers a twenty-year-old a live casino at midnight gets a tax line in the budget.

The maths they skip in the ad

Betting odds are brutal.

On a standard sports bet you need to win about 52.4% of the time just to break even. Most people can’t. Only a small share of bettors stay profitable long term.

Parlays push the house edge into double digits. Keep betting long enough and the edge shifts your money to the operator.

Prediction markets work the same way. Most of the volume is still sport. Oversight sits close to zero.

Bitcoin went the other way. Over the past decade it averaged annual returns of around 60%.

Modest sums became multiples. Crashes included. Everyone remembers the crashes.

Volatile, yes. But if you simply held it, there was no negative expected value baked in.

Guess which one got the regulatory spotlight.

The product with a built-in house edge got ad slots, tax revenue and silence.

Call it what it is

Some countries blocked prediction markets. Belgium, France, Italy, Poland, Spain. The wider pattern didn’t budge.

Outlets that lecture you about Bitcoin print betting odds every night. Governments collect. Regulators “protect” you from crypto. The real risk walks around free.

Owning a scarce digital asset gets called gambling. A betting app aimed at teenagers gets called harmless fun.

€128 billion in Europe. $166.9 billion in American sports bets. Young men eat the losses. Governments cash the cheques.

Call it incompetence if you want. After watching my nephew tap “deposit” again, I stopped giving them the benefit of the doubt.
