Banks fall to passwords. Bitcoin falls to people.

Bitcoin sits near $80,200, down about 2% on the week. The main story was not price: Korea is investigating an AI-assisted attack on seven financial firms and around 66,000 people.

By Ras Vasilisin Markets desk 26 Oct 2026 · 09:50 UTC Block — 3 min
Share

On Monday morning Bitcoin sits at roughly $80,200, down about 2% on the week. On Wednesday it climbed to $84,840; on Saturday it dropped to $79,120. US Bitcoin ETFs took in $301 million after two weeks of outflows, but Thursday brought an outflow of $635.6 million.

Price was not the main story, though. In Korea, police are investigating an AI-assisted attack on seven financial firms and around 66,000 people. In Europe, Unit 42 described a bank opened up by a third-party gateway with weak login. The question came right away: if they can hack a bank, can they hack Bitcoin? They can’t. Here is what happened.

1. Price and ETFs

The week opened near $82,000. On Wednesday Bitcoin rose to $84,840, then slipped below $81,000 after Tesla and Meta earnings. On Saturday it hit $79,121 on Kraken. On Monday it is around $80,200, roughly 2% below 18 October. The $80,000 floor held, but only just. Price is still 1.19× the 200-week moving average ($67,482), about 19% above the long-term floor. Fear & Greed reads a neutral 50.

After two weeks of outflows (almost a billion), flows turned. US spot Bitcoin ETFs took in $301.2 million according to Farside; BlackRock’s IBIT alone took $565.7 million. But Thursday saw $635.6 million leave, and Friday $137.9 million (preliminary). Strategy bought only 267 BTC for $22.1 million, demand went through ETFs.

2. Korea: 66,000 people, no core

Police are investigating an attack on seven firms. Data on about 66,000 people leaked: Shinhan 25,729 customers (names, phone numbers, incomes, credit limits, in some cases national ID numbers), KB 119, Hana 89, plus BNK Busan, two savings banks and Hyundai Capital. Woori and NH NongHyup stopped the attacks.

According to CrowdStrike, the attacker used ARTEX and language models via Claude Code. President Lee Jae-myung said AI was likely involved. Police assigned 28 investigators. Nobody lost savings: the hacker came in through side doors (intermediaries, a mobile system for employees), not through online banking or the core system.

3. Europe: someone else’s gate

Unit 42 at Palo Alto Networks described a breach at an unnamed European bank. The attacker came in through a gateway run by an outside firm without proper login protection. With stolen passwords, they scanned the network for months, guessed admin passwords and tried to take over the server that pushes updates across the whole bank. That is where they were stopped.

Here, too, nobody broke any encryption. A badly locked door at a vendor was enough. The bank then reset all compromised passwords.

4. Why this is not an attack on Bitcoin

On 23 October Changpeng Zhao wrote that AI doesn’t need to break Bitcoin’s cryptography; leaked data is enough for better phishing. The first part holds; the claim that banks use weaker encryption was not backed up by anyone.

Bitcoin has no bank-style side doors. Coins move only with a private-key signature; nobody has broken that cryptography since 2009. Where people actually lose bitcoin:

  • Phishing. On 17 October someone hijacked NGRAVE LIQUID notifications. About 70 wallets lost $2.56 million, $1.77 million of it in bitcoin.
  • The device. In the Ledger case, a group fitted seed-sniffing hardware into genuine wallets.
  • The exchange. Coins on an exchange are a promise, and an exchange has the same side doors as Shinhan.

5. What it means and what to watch

Three things. First, $80,000 held only just; the next support is around $77,500. Second, one green ETF week is not yet a streak. Third, the leaked Korean data is fuel for phishing. It won’t threaten the network, it will threaten people who click.

Watch Farside flows, Strategy’s Monday purchases, and the $80,000 / $77,500 levels. Buy hardware wallets directly from the manufacturer. Never type your seed anywhere. Treat any alert that pushes you to act fast as an attack.
