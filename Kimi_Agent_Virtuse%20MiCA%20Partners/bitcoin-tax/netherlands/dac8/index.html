Your exchange is reporting your Bitcoin: DAC8 in the Netherlands

Since 1 January 2026, exchanges record the identity, tax ID and transactions of Dutch residents under DAC8. Dutch providers report 2026 data to the Belastingdienst by 31 January 2027, before your return is due on 1 May 2027. Bitcoin is taxed in Box 3 on its value on 1 January, not when you sell.

What DAC8 is

DAC8 (EU Directive 2023/2226) brings crypto into the automatic exchange of tax information that already covers bank accounts. The Netherlands implemented it with the act on exchange of crypto-asset data (Staatsblad 2026, 79), in force from 11 April 2026 with retroactive effect to 1 January 2026.

Dutch providers report to the Belastingdienst for Dutch and other EU customers alike. Exchanges licensed in other EU countries report to their own tax authority, which forwards the data to the Netherlands.

What your exchange reports

ReportedDetails
Who you areName, address, date of birth and tax identification number
Crypto to euro or other moneyTotal amounts bought and sold per crypto-asset and year
Crypto to cryptoTotal amounts per crypto-asset
TransfersTransfers out of the exchange, including to your own wallet

Moving Bitcoin between your own wallets is not taxed, but a transfer out of an exchange shows up in the report. Keep a record that the address is yours.

Unlike in most countries, data from Dutch providers arrives before you file. The Belastingdienst can compare it with your return from the start.

How Bitcoin is taxed in the Netherlands

  • Bitcoin counts as other assets in Box 3. You are taxed on a deemed return on its value on 1 January, not on what you actually gain when you sell.
  • For 2026 the deemed return on other assets is 6%, taxed at 36%.
  • The tax-free allowance for 2026 is €59,357 per person, or €118,714 for fiscal partners.
  • Selling or swapping Bitcoin is not a taxable event in Box 3, and losses cannot be offset.
  • If your actual return was lower than the deemed return, you can file a statement of actual return and be taxed on the lower amount. For crypto, unrealised price changes count.
  • A move to taxing actual returns passed the Tweede Kamer in February 2026 but is not law; its planned 2028 start is uncertain.
  • Active or professional trading can be taxed in Box 1 instead; ask a tax advisor.

What to do now

  1. Note the value of all your crypto on 1 January 2026, including coins held on foreign exchanges and in your own wallet.
  2. Declare it in Box 3 on your 2026 return by 1 May 2027.
  3. If 2026 was a bad year, compare your actual return with the deemed 6% and consider the statement of actual return.
  4. If you left crypto out of earlier returns, talk to a tax advisor about a voluntary correction before the Belastingdienst finds it.
  5. When you withdraw to your own wallet, keep proof that the address belongs to you.

FAQ

Do I pay tax when I sell Bitcoin in the Netherlands?

Not as a private investor. Box 3 taxes a deemed return on your holdings on 1 January, so selling or swapping is not itself taxed.

Is my hardware wallet reported?

No. Your wallet is not reported, but transfers from an exchange to it are. Coins in your own wallet still belong in Box 3.

The Netherlands passed DAC8 late. Is 2026 still reported?

Yes. The act applies retroactively from 1 January 2026.

Will Box 3 tax actual gains instead?

A bill to do so passed the lower house in 2026, but it is not law yet and its planned 2028 start may slip.
