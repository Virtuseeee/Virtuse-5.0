Your exchange is reporting your Bitcoin trades: DAC8 in Slovakia

Since 1 January 2026, crypto exchanges record the identity, tax ID and trades of Slovak residents under DAC8. Your 2026 tax return is due on 31 March 2027, but data from all EU exchanges reaches the Financial Administration by 30 September 2027, after you file. Slovakia has no holding-period exemption: every Bitcoin sale or swap is taxable.

What DAC8 is

DAC8 (EU Directive 2023/2226) brings crypto into the automatic exchange of tax information that already covers bank accounts. Slovakia implemented it by amending the Act on Automatic Exchange of Financial Account Information (Act No. 359/2015), with effect from 1 January 2026.

Most exchanges Slovaks use are licensed in another EU country. They report to that country's tax authority, which forwards the data to Slovakia. Providers reporting in Slovakia send it to the Financial Administration directly.

What your exchange reports

ReportedDetails
Who you areName, address, date of birth and tax identification number
Crypto to euroTotal amounts bought and sold per crypto-asset and year
Crypto to cryptoTotal amounts per crypto-asset
TransfersTransfers out of the exchange, including to your own wallet

Moving Bitcoin between your own wallets is not taxed, but a transfer out of an exchange shows up in the report. Keep a record that the address is yours.

The data arrives after most people have filed. A missing or understated gain can surface months after the deadline.

How Bitcoin is taxed in Slovakia

  • Income from selling crypto is other income under section 8 of the Income Tax Act.
  • Taxable: selling for euros, swapping for another crypto-asset, and paying for goods or services. Buying, holding and moving coins between your own wallets are not taxed.
  • There is no holding-period exemption. A 7% rate and a one-year exemption were passed in 2023, but the consolidation package cancelled them before they took effect.
  • Income tax for 2026 on your total tax base: 19% up to €43,975, 25% up to €60,388, 30% up to €74,997 and 35% above.
  • Health insurance contributions of 16% are charged on the gain on top of income tax.
  • You can deduct the purchase price and fees, but only up to the income. Gains and losses from crypto in the same year offset each other; a net loss cannot reduce other income or be carried forward.
  • Crypto income goes on the type B personal income tax return.

What to do now

  1. Export your full trade history from every exchange you have used, including closed accounts.
  2. Match each sale and swap with its purchase price and fees, including crypto-to-crypto swaps.
  3. File a type B return by 31 March 2027 and count the 16% health contributions in your budget.
  4. If you did not declare gains in earlier years, talk to a tax advisor about a supplementary return. Correcting a return before a tax audit starts is treated more leniently than a finding during one.
  5. When you withdraw to your own wallet, keep proof that the address belongs to you.

FAQ

Do I pay tax when I swap Bitcoin for another crypto-asset?

Yes. In Slovakia a crypto-to-crypto swap counts as a sale, and the gain is taxed in that year.

Is my hardware wallet reported?

No. Your wallet's balance is not reported, but transfers from an exchange to it are.

Does it help to hold Bitcoin longer than a year?

Not for Bitcoin held directly. Slovakia has no holding-period exemption for crypto. Securities traded on a regulated market, such as some Bitcoin ETPs, follow different rules; ask a tax advisor.

I bought before 2026. Does that matter?

DAC8 data starts with 2026, but the Financial Administration can still assess earlier years within the statutory period.
