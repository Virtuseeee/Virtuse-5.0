# Dimension 3: SOM — Serviceable Obtainable Market for a New Bitcoin-Only Entrant in Europe

**Research Date**: May 29, 2026
**Focus**: Bitcoin-only services (non-custodial, self-custody, P2P, open-source) in Europe (EU + EFTA + UK), excluding MiCA-regulated CASPs
**Time Horizon**: 2-3 years for new entrant

---

## Executive Summary

The Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM) for a new Bitcoin-only entrant in Europe represents a realistic revenue opportunity of **€1.8M–€4.5M in Year 1**, growing to **€5M–€12M by Year 3**, depending on the chosen segment (hardware, software/P2P, or services). These projections are grounded in verified competitor revenues, market penetration benchmarks for fintech startups, and conservative assumptions about customer acquisition in a niche but rapidly growing market.

Key constraints on obtainable share include:
- **Intense hardware competition**: Ledger + Trezor control ~60% of the hardware wallet market [^1^]
- **Network effects in P2P trading**: liquidity begets liquidity, disadvantaging new entrants
- **Customer acquisition costs**: $85–$150 per user in the crypto/DeFi space [^2^]
- **Brand trust requirements**: security-sensitive users prefer established, audited brands

## 4. SOM Calculations by Segment

### Methodology

SOM is calculated using:
1. **Bottom-up**: (Target Customers × ARPU × Expected Penetration Rate)
2. **Top-down validation**: (% of competitor revenue achievable)
3. **Constraint check**: CAC must be < LTV; achievable with available capital

### 4.1 Segment A: Bitcoin Hardware Wallets

**European Bitcoin Hardware Wallet SAM**: ~€36–60M (narrowing global Bitcoin-only estimate to Europe)

**Competitive landscape**:
- Ledger: ~35-40% market share, >$100M revenue
- Trezor: ~20-25% market share, ~$47M revenue
- BitBox: ~5-8% market share (Bitcoin-only specialist)
- Others (Coldcard, SeedSigner, Blockstream Jade): ~10-15%

**Realistic penetration for new entrant**:

| Year | Market Share | Rationale |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| Year 1 | 0.5–1.0% | Building brand, certification, initial distribution |
| Year 2 | 1.5–3.0% | Product-market fit, word-of-mouth, community building |
| Year 3 | 3.0–5.0% | Established brand, retail partnerships, enterprise offering |

**SOM Hardware (Europe)**:

| Year | SOM (Conservative) | SOM (Optimistic) | Assumptions |
|------|-------------------|------------------|-------------|
| Year 1 | €180K–€360K | €360K–€600K | 2K–5K units @ €120 ASP |
| Year 2 | €540K–€1.8M | €1.2M–€3M | 8K–20K units @ €120–150 ASP |
| Year 3 | €1.1M–€3M | €2.4M–€5M | 15K–35K units @ €120–150 ASP |

**Key insight**: Hardware is the hardest segment to penetrate due to:
- High upfront R&D ($250K–$1M for secure element design)
- Certification costs ($50K–$250K per model) [^25^]
- Supply chain complexity and chip shortages
- Ledger/Trezor brand dominance and retail distribution

## 6. Key Constraints & Risk Factors

### 6.1 Competitive Moats

| Competitor | Moat Strength | Description |
|------------|--------------|-------------|
| Ledger | Very Strong | Brand, distribution, $100B+ AUM, retail presence, enterprise partnerships |
| Trezor | Strong | Open-source reputation, 11-year track record, Czech/EU manufacturing |
| BitBox | Moderate | Bitcoin-only focus, Swiss quality positioning, open-source |
| Peach | Moderate | Mobile-first, no-KYC, Swiss regulatory arbitrage |
| BTCPay | Strong (ecosystem) | Network effects, open-source community, merchant lock-in |

### 6.2 New Entrant Challenges

1. **Trust deficit**: Security-sensitive users prefer established brands with audit history
2. **Distribution**: Ledger/Trezor available on Amazon, retail stores; new entrants struggle for shelf space
3. **Certification**: Hardware wallets require EAL5+ certification (€50K–€250K, 6–12 months) [^25^]
4. **MiCA compliance**: Even non-custodial services face regulatory uncertainty
5. **CAC vs LTV**: At $85–150 CAC and low hardware margins, unit economics are challenging
6. **Network effects**: P2P platforms require liquidity to attract users

### 6.3 Favorable Trends

1. **Self-custody wave**: 56.6% of users prefer self-custody wallets [^26^]
2. **Hardware wallet penetration <15%**: Massive room for growth [^10^]
3. **European crypto adoption growing**: 50M holders → 140M wallet users [^3^][^4^]
4. **Bitcoin dominance in Europe**: 38% of crypto market, most traded asset [^7^]
5. **Non-custodial wallet growth**: 47% YoY growth in self-custody wallets [^26^]
6. **Ledger IPO**: Validates market size, increases awareness, creates talent pool

## 8. Conclusion & Recommendations

### For a New Bitcoin-Only Entrant in Europe:

**Realistic SOM Range (Year 3)**: **€4M–€15M** depending on segment and execution

**Optimal entry strategy**:
1. **Start with services** (BTCPay hosting, consulting) for immediate revenue
2. **Build software** (P2P app or Bitcoin tooling) for scalable growth
3. **Consider hardware only after** achieving software/services scale ($5M+ revenue)

**Critical success factors**:
- Bitcoin-only positioning differentiates from Ledger/Trezor multi-coin
- European manufacturing (German/Swiss) commands premium
- Open-source builds trust with core Bitcoin demographic
- Regulatory clarity on non-custodial operations
- Community-first marketing (Bitcoin conferences, podcasts, NOSTR)

**Capital requirements**:
- Software/services entry: €200K–€500K
- Hardware entry: €1M–€3M (R&D + certification + inventory)
