When you can get the money

Second pillar
It is paid out in retirement, under rules set by law.
Mutual funds and ETFs
A mutual fund pays out units under its own statute; an ETF is sold on an exchange. There may be an exit charge.
Bitcoin

Tax

Second pillar

Mutual funds and ETFs
Income from mutual funds and ETFs is taxed under its own rules.
Bitcoin
A gain on selling Bitcoin is taxed under its own rules.

Bitcoin

Returns are in euros, after the fund's ongoing costs, without entry or exit charges. Ongoing costs per year are management and other administrative or operating costs plus transaction costs, as stated in the key information document. The entry charge is the maximum in that document; the exit charge applies when selling after one year. Bitcoin is shown on the day most manager reports share.

Methodology and sources

Second-pillar fund returns are the official figures from the weekly report of the Slovak Association of Pension Fund Management Companies on the data date: 1 year, and 5 and 10 years as an average per year. The pension unit value is already net of the management fee.

Bitcoin: its euro price on the same day as the funds and on the same day 1, 5 and 10 years earlier. The price is CoinMetrics PriceUSD converted at the European Central Bank rate of the same day, as on Bitcoin monthly returns on this site. Average per year = (end / start) to the power 1/years, minus 1. For the second pillar the day is 6 November 2026; for mutual funds and ETFs it is the day most manager reports in that group share, which is why Bitcoin's 1-year return differs between the two views.

Return after inflation = (1 + nominal return) / (1 + inflation) − 1, for each period separately. Inflation is Slovakia's harmonised index of consumer prices (compiled by the Statistical Office of the Slovak Republic, published by Eurostat): the latest published month and the same month 1, 5 and 10 years earlier. The same series for every row.

Largest drawdown and volatility: weekly values (Fridays) over the last 10 years; for the funds the pension unit value from the National Bank of Slovakia, for Bitcoin its euro price on the same Friday. Drawdown is the largest fall from a previous high. Volatility is the standard deviation of weekly log returns times the square root of 52. Weekly values miss moves within a week, for the funds and for Bitcoin alike.

Second-pillar fees: since 1 August 2026, Act No. 43/2004 Coll. lets a pension company charge only a management fee, at most 0.4% a year (§ 63(1) and § 63a(3)). The law no longer has a performance fee. On 18 November 2026 we checked the actual rate in the current statute of each of the 17 funds: every one is 0.4% of the fund's average annual preliminary net asset value. Each fund links to its statute. The fund also pays a depositary fee under its statute; the returns on this page are already net of all these costs. Bitcoin is not a fund and has no management fee; buying, selling and storing it can still cost something, depending on where and how you hold it.

For NN Index Global, the series up to 31 October 2019 is that of AEGON INDEX, which merged into it according to the National Bank of Slovakia's record. The association gives Index Global the same founding date, 24 April 2012.

Mutual funds and ETFs: returns come from the manager's monthly report (quarterly for the real estate fund) for that share class in euros, after the fund's ongoing costs, without entry or exit charges. Where the manager prints only a cumulative 5 or 10-year return, we convert it to an average per year. Costs come only from the key information document of that share class. The exit charge is that document's figure for leaving after one year; selling earlier can cost more. Reports have different dates, end of September or October 2026; the date is next to each fund.

How we chose the funds: we have no official figures for how much of each fund is sold in Slovakia. So we used a simple rule: the largest retail funds of four big Slovak managers (Erste, Tatra, Eurizon, IAD) by fund assets, foreign funds of large managers whose euro share class has a key information document in Slovak, and the largest broad ETFs by assets. It is not a ranking or a recommendation, and the list is not complete. We did not use any seller's ratings or stars.

Chart and slider: for the second pillar the chart uses weekly pension unit values and Bitcoin prices. One deposit grows with those values; with monthly contributions, a deposit is added on the first Friday of each month. For mutual funds and ETFs we have no weekly series, so the chart shows only the value at the end of the period from the return per year in the manager's report. Everything is before tax and without entry charges.

Frequently asked questions

Where do the fund figures come from?

Second-pillar returns come from the weekly report of the Slovak Association of Pension Fund Management Companies. We compute drawdown and volatility from weekly values (Fridays) over the last 10 years: for the funds the pension unit value from the National Bank of Slovakia, for Bitcoin its euro price on the same Friday. For mutual funds and ETFs, returns come from the managers' reports and costs from the key information documents. Every figure has its source and date in the data file.

What is the return after inflation?

It is the return adjusted for rising prices: (1 + return) / (1 + inflation) - 1. If a fund made 3% while prices rose 4%, you lost a little in real terms, even though the nominal figure is positive.

Why is a figure missing for some funds?

The fund started after the period begins, or the official source does not publish that figure. For some ETFs the issuer publishes returns only in US dollars. We don't fill a missing figure from other funds, from a shorter period or by converting currencies.

Why is there no fee for Bitcoin?

Bitcoin is not a fund, so it has no statutory management fee. The cost of buying, selling and storing it depends on the provider and how you hold it. We have no single verifiable public figure that fits everyone, so we don't show a zero. Our Bitcoin Fee Index compares providers' fees.

Why do some ETFs show no return?

The issuers of these ETFs publish returns only in US dollars, the fund's base currency. Converting them to euros would be our own calculation, not an official figure, so we don't show one. Their costs from the key information document are shown.
