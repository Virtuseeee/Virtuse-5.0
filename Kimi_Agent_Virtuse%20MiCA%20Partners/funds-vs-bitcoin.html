Returns are in euros, after the fund's ongoing costs, without entry or exit charges. Ongoing costs per year are management and other administrative or operating costs plus transaction costs, as stated in the KID. The entry charge is the maximum in the KID; a distributor may charge less. Bitcoin is shown on the same day as most factsheets. We don't show drawdown and volatility for these funds because we have no official daily series of the unit price. How we chose the funds is in the methodology.

What the figures don't tell you

The second pillar is Slovakia's statutory old-age pension saving. The money is not freely available: it is paid out in retirement, under rules set by law. You don't buy a fund like a share on an exchange; the Social Insurance Agency pays into it from your contributions.

Guaranteed bond funds carry an individual guarantee set by law. Their low return comes with that guarantee; it is not a mistake in the table.

Bitcoin is not a pension fund. It has no statutory guarantee, no one supervises it the way the National Bank of Slovakia supervises pension companies, and its price can fall by more than half in a short time, as the drawdown column shows. Its legal and tax treatment is different too: a gain on sale is taxed, while second-pillar payouts follow their own rules.

So this page doesn't say what to choose. It puts a few public figures side by side so anyone can form their own view.

Methodology and sources

Fund returns are the official figures from the weekly report of the Slovak Association of Pension Fund Management Companies (ADSS) on the data date: 1 year, and 5 and 10 years as an average per year. The pension unit value is already net of the management fee.

Bitcoin: its euro price on the same day as the funds and on the same day 1, 5 and 10 years earlier. The price is CoinMetrics PriceUSD converted at the European Central Bank rate of the same day, as on Bitcoin monthly returns on this site. Average per year = (end / start) to the power 1/years, minus 1.

Return after inflation = (1 + nominal return) / (1 + inflation) - 1, for each period separately. Inflation is Slovakia's harmonised index of consumer prices (HICP, compiled by the Statistical Office of the Slovak Republic, published by Eurostat): the latest published month and the same month 1, 5 and 10 years earlier. The same series for every row.

Largest drawdown and volatility: weekly values (Fridays) over the last 10 years; for the funds the pension unit value from the National Bank of Slovakia, for Bitcoin its euro price on the same Friday. Drawdown is the largest fall from a previous high. Volatility is the standard deviation of weekly log returns times the square root of 52. Weekly values miss moves within a week, for the funds and for Bitcoin alike.

Fees: since 1 August 2026, Act No. 43/2004 Coll. lets a pension company charge only a management fee, at most 0.4% a year (§ 63(1) and § 63a(3)). The law no longer has a performance fee. Bitcoin is not a fund and has no management fee; buying, selling and storing it can still cost something, depending on where and how you hold it.

For NN Index Global, the series up to 31 October 2019 is that of AEGON INDEX, which merged into it according to the National Bank of Slovakia's record. ADSS gives Index Global the same founding date, 24 April 2012.

Mutual funds and ETFs: returns come from the manager's factsheet (monthly; quarterly for the real estate fund) for that share class in euros, after the fund's ongoing costs, without entry or exit charges. Where the manager prints only a cumulative 5 or 10-year return, we convert it to an average per year. Costs come only from the key information document (KID) of that share class. The exit charge is the KID figure for leaving after one year; selling earlier can cost more. Factsheets have different dates, end of September or October 2026; the date is next to each fund, and the data file computes Bitcoin separately for each date.

How we chose the funds: we have no official figures for how much of each fund is sold in Slovakia. So we used a simple rule: the largest retail funds of four big Slovak managers (Erste, Tatra, Eurizon, IAD) by fund assets, foreign funds of large managers whose euro share class has a key information document in Slovak, and the largest broad ETFs by assets. It is not a ranking or a recommendation, and the list is not complete. We did not use any seller's ratings or stars.

Frequently asked questions

Where do the fund figures come from?

Returns come from the weekly report of the Slovak Association of Pension Fund Management Companies (ADSS). We compute drawdown and volatility from the daily pension unit values published by the National Bank of Slovakia. Every figure has its source and date in the data file.

What is the return after inflation?

It is the return adjusted for rising prices: (1 + return) / (1 + inflation) - 1. If a fund made 3% while prices rose 4%, you lost a little in real terms, even though the nominal figure is positive.

Why does a fund show n/a?

The fund started after the period begins, or the official source does not publish that figure. We don't fill a missing figure from other funds or from a shorter period.

Why is Bitcoin's fee n/a?

Bitcoin is not a fund, so it has no statutory management fee. The cost of buying, selling and storing it depends on the provider and how you hold it. We have no single verifiable public figure that fits everyone, so we don't show a zero.

Why do some ETFs show no return?

The issuers of these ETFs publish returns only in US dollars, the fund's base currency. Converting them to euros would be our own calculation, not an official figure, so we don't show one. Their costs from the key information document are shown.
