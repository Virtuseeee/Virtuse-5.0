Why the Financial System Treats You Like a 17th Century Peasant

By Ras Vasilisin Custody desk 7 Sept 2026 · 18:41 UTC Block — 3 min
Share

I’ve got every exam, certification, and qualification you can imagine behind me.

Yet every time I try to open a brokerage account for myself, I hit the same forms and quizzes as a total beginner. I spend days proving I deserve to manage my own money.

Eventually they approve the account. But it always feels like someone else is better qualified to judge that than me.

It took me a while to see the real point.

There’s an old, timeless rule about power. To control someone, you don’t need chains. You just need to convince them they’re not smart enough to run their own life.

Sounds like a conspiracy theory. It isn’t.

It’s documented history. Just wearing a new costume.

When They Burned the Bible So You Wouldn’t Think for Yourself

The year is 1620. The Battle of White Mountain.

After the failed Estates uprising, the Habsburgs took over the country. They launched a brutal re-Catholicization of Bohemia and Slovakia.

One of their first moves was destroying the Bible of Kralice. It was the first complete Czech translation of Scripture, made straight from the original languages.

Soldiers and Jesuits searched homes. They burned every Bible they found, right in the town square. Reading Scripture in your own language became a crime.

The stated reason sounded noble, as always: protect the faithful from heresy and misinterpretation. Mass had to go back to Latin.

Anyone who wanted to know Scripture had to go through a Catholic priest. He’d explain it to them, in line with the official interpretation, of course.

Protecting the faithful wasn’t really the point.

Restoring a monopoly on truth was.

Latin Has a Different Name Today

Fast-forward four centuries. The cassock got traded for a suit. The mechanism stayed exactly the same.

Try opening an account today to invest your own, honestly earned money. First you wade through a maze of questionnaires: your income, your education, your net worth. Under the banner of “investor protection,” people in suits decide where you can put your savings, and where you can’t.

Qualified investor status is the modern Latin. Without it, you don’t get anywhere near the good stuff.

Add mandatory KYC (Know Your Customer) and AML (Anti-Money Laundering) checks on top. Now you’re proving where every euro came from, like you owe someone an explanation for your own work.

And once you finally clear the paperwork, one more obstacle is waiting.

The biggest one.

The Chosen Few: 1.5%

As a rule, unless you have at least 1 million euros in assets, you can’t invest in most financial products.

So the questionnaire mostly lets the wealthy through. Banks call them high-net-worth or private investors. According to the latest Global Wealth Report from UBS, only 1.5% of the world’s adults qualify. That’s the direct successor to the old Credit Suisse series, by the way.

The remaining 98.5%, or “non-accredited investors” as the banks call them, have barely any real options.

Governments claim they’re protecting 98.5% of us by locking us out of the system. That’s not protection. That’s discrimination.

It’s the exact same logic as banning 98.5% of people from reading the Bible in 1620. Their crime? Not having the “proper education” to understand God’s word.

Then and now, it’s the same test. Judge whether someone “understands” something by their bank balance, and you’ve judged nothing at all.

Bitcoin: The Translation No One Can Ban

Bitcoin isn’t a gadget. Think of it as the direct heir to the printing press and vernacular Bible translations.

It’s the first asset in history that needs no one in a cassock or a suit to sign off on the truth.

The protocol doesn’t know your name. It doesn’t check your net worth. It doesn’t set conditions.

Its rules apply the same way to a Wall Street billionaire and to someone with no bank account at all, anywhere on earth. If you hold your own private keys, you don’t need anyone’s permission to send a single transaction.

Not 1.5% of the population.

Anyone.

History has taught us one clear rule: real freedom shows up the moment the middleman becomes unnecessary. Breaking the monopoly on reading Scripture redrew the power map of Central Europe. It helped spark the Renaissance.

Bitcoin is doing the same thing to money.
