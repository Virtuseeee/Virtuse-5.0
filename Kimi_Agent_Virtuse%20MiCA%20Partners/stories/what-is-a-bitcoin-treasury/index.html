What is a Bitcoin Treasury

By Ras Vasilisin Treasury desk 12 Sept 2025 · 14:54 UTC Block — 3 min
Share

Hedging, Investment and Capital Growth

A Bitcoin treasury refers to a company holding a portion of its assets in Bitcoin. This strategy is an increasingly common way for businesses to manage their cash and investments, offering a modern alternative to inflation protection, diversification, and potential long-term growth.

For European firms, holding Bitcoin can serve three primary functions:

  • Operational Needs: More than 15 000 businesses worldwide accept Bitcoin for payment. Companies that accept Bitcoin for payments as part of their business operations can organically accumulate it on their balance sheet. This can expand their customer base to include a growing segment of digital asset holders, particularly in sectors like e-commerce, travel, and luxury goods.

 

  • Currency Hedging and Diversification: As a decentralised, non-sovereign asset, Bitcoin is not tied to any single government or central bank. This makes it a potential hedge against fiat currency devaluation and inflation, a key concern for multinational companies. For a firm in a country concerned about local currency volatility or a business seeking to diversify away from traditional assets, Bitcoin can serve as a store of value.

  • Investment: Many corporate leaders view Bitcoin as a strategic investment with the potential for significant long-term appreciation. By allocating a portion of their treasury to Bitcoin, companies are betting on the continued growth and adoption of this digital asset.

Key Considerations for European Businesses

While the potential benefits are significant, European companies must navigate a specific set of regulatory and operational complexities when managing a Bitcoin treasury:

  • Regulatory and Compliance Risks: The European Union is at the forefront of crypto regulation with the Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) framework. This legislation aims to create a harmonised regulatory environment for crypto-asset service providers (CASPs) across the EU. While providing clarity, companies must ensure their practices align with these new rules, as well as with existing anti-money laundering (AML) and counter-terrorist financing (CTF) directives.
  • Accounting Challenges: Accounting standards for cryptocurrencies are still evolving. Businesses must ensure they are compliant with international standards, such as the International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS), which may require special treatment for digital assets.
  • Security and Custody: Unlike funds held in a bank, Bitcoin requires robust security measures to prevent theft or loss. This is why many institutions in Europe choose to use qualified custodians who specialise in securing digital assets. These providers offer institutional-grade solutions like multi-signature wallets and both “hot” (online) and “cold” (offline) storage to ensure assets are protected.
  • Liquidity and Market Risk: The Bitcoin market is volatile and does not have the same centralised market makers as traditional stock exchanges. Companies must plan for market fluctuations and have a strategy for managing liquidity and risk, including potential hedging strategies.
  • Governance: Because blockchain transactions are irreversible, strong internal governance is crucial. Companies must establish clear policies and controls to prevent fraud and unauthorised transactions.

 

Securing Bitcoin Treasuries with Qualified Custody

To mitigate risks, European businesses are increasingly turning to professional custody solutions. A qualified custodian provides a high level of security and professional management, ensuring that assets are stored safely and are only accessible by authorised representatives.

A reputable custodian should offer:

  • A combination of hot and cold storage to balance security with operational needs.
  • Multi-signature wallets that require authorisation from multiple parties for transactions.
  • Role-based access policies to ensure only specific users can manage and move funds.
