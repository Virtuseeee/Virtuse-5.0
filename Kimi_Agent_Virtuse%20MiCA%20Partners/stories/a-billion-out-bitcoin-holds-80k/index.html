A Billion Out, Bitcoin Holds $80K

US spot Bitcoin ETFs lost nearly a billion dollars in two weeks and hot inflation pushed the price below $80,000 for a few hours. Buyers still brought it back.

By Ras Vasilisin Markets desk 19 Oct 2026 · 09:58 UTC Block — 5 min
Share

Bitcoin’s at about $82,500, down about 2% on the week.

US spot Bitcoin ETFs lost nearly a billion dollars in two weeks. US inflation came in hotter than expected and on Thursday pushed the price below $80,000 for a few hours.

Buyers still brought it back. And the network quietly hit a new milestone in the meantime: more than 1 ZH/s of computing power. Here’s what happened and what the coming week depends on.

1. The $80K Floor Held

The week started at its high. Early Monday Bitcoin reached about $84,250, and it didn’t trade higher for the rest of the week.

Then came two chip-stock selloffs in Seoul. The first pulled it just under $81,000, the second took it toward $80,100 overnight from Wednesday into Thursday.

The worst came on Thursday afternoon European time, a little under an hour and a half after the US producer price release. Bitcoin fell to $79,709 on Kraken and $79,676 on Coinbase, its lowest in almost a month.

It didn’t stay below $80,000 for long. By Friday morning it was back near $82,800, and over the weekend it held between $80,700 and $82,200. On Monday morning it’s around $82,500.

Leverage made the drop worse. In the 24 hours around Thursday, nearly $950 million of positions were liquidated, about 90% of them bets on a rise (CoinGlass). (A liquidation is when an exchange force-closes a trade made with borrowed money because the trader ran out of collateral.)

2. Nearly A Billion Out Of ETFs

US spot Bitcoin ETFs lost $228.4 million last week by Farside’s count (SoSoValue has $228.6 million). The week before, it was about $679 million.

That’s roughly $910 million out over two weeks.

It was a choppy week. Monday minus $65 million, Tuesday plus $176 million, Wednesday, inflation day, minus $261 million. Thursday brought in just $5 million and Friday sent another $82 million out.

October is about $615 million in the red so far, according to Decrypt. Since launching in January 2024, these funds still hold $57.3 billion in cumulative net inflows.

One line matters here. The average ETF holder bought at around $82,300, according to a Nexo analyst (Glassnode puts it at $81,500). On Thursday the price was below it. The average ETF holder was briefly underwater, and that’s exactly when selling comes easiest.

3. Inflation Is Back

Why did they sell? Two numbers.

On Wednesday, September consumer prices came out at 3.7% year over year, against 3.6% expected and 3.4% in August. Month over month, prices rose 0.6%, mostly because of gasoline.

On Thursday came producer prices. They jumped 1.3% in a month, the most since March 2022, and 6.3% year over year, led by energy.

The 10-year Treasury yield ended the week at 5.09%. (The yield is what the US government pays to borrow money for ten years.) Brent crude climbed to $109.86 a barrel on Friday, its highest since May.

When inflation rises, the Fed leans toward hiking. And when a safe bond pays more than 5%, holding an asset that pays nothing has a real cost.

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh gave no hint in Bangkok of what the Fed will do at its 27–28 October meeting.

4. Who Was Buying Anyway

The biggest corporate buyer of Bitcoin bought little this time.

Over the past two weeks Strategy bought just 584 BTC for $50.2 million. In the same period it spent $368.7 million buying back its STRC preferred shares. It holds 848,250 BTC at an average cost of $75,444.

The next blow came from MSCI. It approved rules under which so-called non-operating companies can be dropped from its indexes, and a simulation put Strategy and Metaplanet among them. The list comes out on 11 November and the changes take effect on 30 November. Strategy’s stock fell 9.6% on Friday. That doesn’t mean automatic removal or forced Bitcoin sales, but it could cost the stock some passive buyers.

Smaller Strive bought more: 6,133 BTC over two weeks, bringing it to 35,595.

And miners didn’t wait for the price at all. On Friday, 16 October, mining difficulty rose 5.92% to 140.58T, its highest since March. The network’s hashrate is above 1,000 EH/s, or 1 ZH/s, with mempool.space showing about 1,022 EH/s over three days.

Earning is getting harder. Daily revenue per PH/s fell to $36.75. Machines keep joining the network anyway.

Short-term money sold. Miners and patient buyers added.

5. What It Means For The Price

Three things.

First, $80,000 held. Glassnode says Thursday’s loss-selling may have marked a local bottom. It sees the next support at $77,500 to $78,000.

Second, the average ETF cost basis. As long as Bitcoin stays above roughly $82,300, the average ETF holder is in profit and selling pressure eases. Below it, the pressure builds.

Third, inflation and the Fed. Softer numbers or a clear signal that the Fed won’t hike would make holding Bitcoin cheaper again. The opposite would make it more expensive.

Until ETF flows turn into a streak of green days, Bitcoin is likely to keep reacting to every inflation print and every move in yields.

6. What To Watch This Week

  • Monday 19 Oct: Strategy usually discloses its purchases on Mondays. Watch whether it bought more Bitcoin than STRC shares.
  • Wednesday 21 Oct, 19:00 CEST: a $13 billion US 20-year Treasury auction. Weak demand could push yields up.
  • Sunday 25 Oct: Europe switches to winter time. Until 1 November, US times will be one hour closer.
  • Tuesday and Wednesday 27–28 Oct: Fed meeting. On Wednesday at 15:00 CET, Strategy shareholders vote on daily STRC dividends.
  • Every day: ETF flows on Farside.
  • Levels: $80,000 is the floor to hold. $82,300 is the line above which the average ETF holder is in profit.

The next difficulty adjustment comes around 30 October. Mempool.space currently estimates it at minus 2.4%, and that estimate will still change.
