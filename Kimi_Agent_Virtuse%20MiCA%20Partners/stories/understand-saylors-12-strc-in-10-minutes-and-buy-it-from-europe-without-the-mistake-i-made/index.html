Understand Saylor’s 12% STRC in 10 Minutes, and Buy It From Europe Without the Mistake I Made

By Ras Vasilisin Policy desk 2 Oct 2026 · 13:06 UTC Block — 7 min
Share

By the end of this article you will know where the 12% comes from, what you really own, how it behaved in a crash, and the exact steps to buy it from the EU.

I have a confession.

In April I published an article on this blog about STRC. It promised 11.5% a year, “8x collateral” and a price “stable as a rock”. I believed every word. I wanted to believe it: a bitcoin-linked income that doesn’t swing is exactly what I’d been looking for.

Two months later STRC fell to about $71. And when I finally read Strategy’s own documents line by line, one sentence stopped me cold: STRC “is not collateralized by Strategy’s bitcoin holdings.” No eight times. No collateral at all.

I wasn’t lying to you. I was repeating a story I liked without checking it. That’s the mistake I don’t want you to make with your own money. So I went back, read the filings, and rebuilt the whole thing from scratch.

Here is the path, in six steps:

  1. Understand where the 12% actually comes from
  2. Know exactly what you own, and what you don’t
  3. Stress-test it before you trust it
  4. Decide whether it fits you
  5. Buy it from the EU in seven moves
  6. Get the tax and timing right

Step 1: Understand where the 12% actually comes from

Bitcoin pays no interest. So the first question is simple: who pays you?

Strategy does. Investors who want a steady dollar income buy STRC. Strategy takes those dollars and buys bitcoin. The dividends come out of Strategy’s own cash: a Board-approved USD Reserve of $5.02 billion, about 3.1 years of preferred dividends and debt interest, plus money it keeps raising by selling shares.

One real week shows the machine at work. In the seven days to 27 September, Strategy sold $246 million of common stock, bought 1,665 bitcoin and paid $22.1 million of preferred dividends from the reserve.

Milestone reached: the 12% is real, but it depends on one company continuing to raise money. Everything else follows from that.

Step 2: Know exactly what you own, and what you don’t

The rate is reviewed every month with one goal: keep STRC near $99–$100. So far it has only gone up.

Now the part I got wrong in April. Strategy holds 847,666 bitcoin, worth about $79 billion on 2 October. It calls the ratio to STRC and everything above it a “BTC Rating” of 6.6x. That number is real. But it is Strategy’s own illustrative metric, “not a rating from any rating agency”, and not one of those coins is pledged to you.

Milestone reached: you own a dollar claim on a company, second in line among its preferred shares. You do not own bitcoin, and you do not gain when bitcoin rises.

Step 3: Stress-test it before you trust it

“Stable as a rock” was my other mistake. Here is what actually happened.

In June 2026 bitcoin dropped to around $64,000–$65,000. STRC closed at $89 on 17 June, its lowest since launch, then fell as low as about $71. Strategy stopped selling new STRC below $100 and, for the first time, sold bitcoin to pay preferred dividends.

It got the price back, but it took work: a bigger USD Reserve, a $2 billion buyback programme aimed first at STRC, and a 12% rate held until the price recovers. By 1 October STRC was at $99.50. Its one-year volatility is still 22%, against roughly 1–8% for the bond funds Strategy compares it with.

The risks to keep in front of you:

  • A long bitcoin winter. If bitcoin stays low for years and Strategy can’t raise money, dividends can be cut or skipped. Owed is not the same as paid.
  • The rate can fall. Strategy can lower it by up to 0.25 percentage points a month (plus any fall in one-month SOFR).
  • Others can jump the queue. Strategy can issue more STRC, or more STRF that ranks above it, without asking you.
  • Capped upside. The price is managed near $100, and Strategy can buy it back at $101.
  • Dollar risk if you spend euros.
  • Rule changes in US tax or securities law.

Milestone reached: you’ve seen it under pressure. It bent hard, then recovered because the company spent heavily to defend it.

Step 4: Decide whether it fits you

Start with the honest math. Strategy’s own example: 1,000 shares cost $98,540 in late September. At 12% they pay $12,000 a year, $500 twice a month, if every dividend is declared and paid.

That figure is in dollars and before tax. And there is no date when you get your $100 back. You exit by selling at whatever the market pays that day.

Then ask yourself one question: what job do I want this money to do?

  • If the job is income, STRC can do it, as long as you treat it like any single-company bet and size it so a bad year doesn’t hurt.
  • If the job is owning bitcoin, STRC is the wrong tool. It gives up bitcoin’s upside for a dollar income and adds a company’s credit risk. Bitcoin in your own wallet has no issuer at all.

Milestone reached: you know which job STRC can do for you, and which one it can’t.

Step 5: Buy it from the EU in seven moves

  1. Pick your route. The real share on Nasdaq (in USD), the same share on Germany’s Tradegate (in EUR), or an exchange-traded product that holds STRC for you.
  2. Open and verify a broker account. ID, address, tax residence.
  3. Sign the W-8BEN form if you buy the US share. Most brokers do it in the app. It lets them apply the tax-treaty rate.
  4. Pass the appropriateness test if asked. Many brokers treat perpetual preferreds as complex products.
  5. Search by ISIN, not ticker: US5949728530. “STRC” also names unrelated products.
  6. Use a limit order. Decide your price before you order; thin European listings can fill far from the US price.
  7. Check how dividends arrive. Cash twice a month for the share; for an ETP, read its distribution policy.

Availability differs by country and account type, so search the ISIN in your own broker’s app first. On 2 October the Tradegate listing showed STRC as suspended, so the euro route may be closed when you read this.

What I’d skip: tokenised “xStocks” on crypto exchanges (a token, not the share, with dividends reinvested) and DeFi vaults that wrap STRC (smart-contract risk on top of Strategy’s). The euro series STRE isn’t offered to EU retail investors at all.

Milestone reached: you have an account, the right ISIN and an order you control.

Step 6: Get the tax and timing right

US tax. Strategy says all of its 2025 preferred distributions were a return of capital for US federal tax purposes, not dividends, and expects the same for at least a decade. A return of capital lowers your cost basis instead of counting as income.

US withholding. A US dividend paid to an EU resident is normally taxed 30% at source, or 15% under most treaties once your W-8BEN is filed. Some brokers withhold first and refund later, when the year’s classification is final. Ask yours how it handles STRC.

Your home country decides its own tax treatment and doesn’t have to follow the US label. Talk to a local tax adviser before you buy, and keep every annual statement.

Timing. Strategy has proposed paying STRC dividends every day instead of twice a month. Shareholders vote on 28 October 2026. If it passes, daily payments start on 2 November. The rate and the total don’t change, only the rhythm. If you hold through a European broker or an ETP, ask whether they’ll pass on daily payments.

Milestone reached: no surprises from the tax office, and you know what changes after 28 October.

TL;DR

STRC pays you 12% to lend your trust to a company that holds bitcoin. That’s a fine deal once you’ve read the terms, and a bad one if you mistake it for owning bitcoin.
